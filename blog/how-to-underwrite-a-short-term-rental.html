How to Underwrite a Short-Term Rental (Airbnb) Deal

Updated June 2026 · 7 min read

"Underwriting" sounds like a banker's word, but it just means one thing: deciding whether a deal is worth your money before you spend it. For a short-term rental (STR), good underwriting is the difference between a property that quietly pays you every month and one that bleeds cash the moment occupancy dips. Here is the framework — and the six numbers that give you a clear BUY or WALK.

Step 1 — Estimate revenue honestly

Two inputs drive STR revenue: ADR (average daily rate) and occupancy. The mistake beginners make is using a listing's best month. Underwrite the normal season instead. Pull comparable active listings, look at a realistic year-round occupancy (often 55–70% in a decent market, not 90%), and multiply: ADR × 365 × occupancy = annual revenue.

Step 2 — Subtract every real cost

STRs have costs long-term rentals don't: cleaning, supplies, utilities, internet, platform fees, dynamic-pricing tools, and management (10–25% if you don't self-manage). Add property tax, insurance (STR insurance costs more), and a maintenance reserve. What's left after costs but before the loan is your net operating income (NOI).

Step 3 — Layer in financing

Subtract annual debt service (mortgage payments) from NOI to get cash flow. Now you can compute the numbers that actually decide the deal.

Step 4 — Stress-test it

A deal that only works in the best case is a gamble, not an investment. Drop occupancy 10 points. Raise the cleaning and management costs. Add a rate hike. If the deal still survives — positive cash flow, DSCR above ~1.2 — you have a real margin of safety. If it falls apart, that's your answer.

The goal of underwriting isn't to make a deal look good. It's to find out, cheaply, whether it actually is.

Common mistakes

  • Using peak-season numbers for the whole year.
  • Forgetting STR-specific costs — cleaning, higher insurance, furnishing, software.
  • Ignoring regulation risk — some cities restrict or ban STRs. Check first.
  • No stress test — always model a worse year.
