Should You Buy a World Cup 2026 Short-Term Rental?

Updated June 2026 · 5 min read

The 2026 World Cup runs across 16 host cities in the US, Canada, and Mexico, and short-term rental prices near the stadiums are climbing fast. The pitch is tempting: buy an Airbnb in a host city, cash in on the tournament. But before you chase the hype, run the math — because a five-week event will not carry a thirty-year mortgage.

The event is upside, not the thesis

The single biggest mistake in event-driven real estate is underwriting the event instead of the property. Tournament demand is a spike. Your loan, taxes, and insurance are there for the other 47 weeks of every year — and for 30 years. So underwrite the property as if the World Cup didn't exist first. If it cash-flows on normal-season numbers, the tournament is a bonus. If it only works because of a five-week event, that's a red flag, not a deal.

Watch the "hype tax"

Sellers in host cities know the World Cup is coming, and many have priced it in. You can easily end up paying 2026 hype prices for 2025 cash flow. The way to avoid it is the same as always: a disciplined model that values the property on its sustainable income, then treats the event weeks as a separate, removable line.

How to underwrite a host-city STR

  1. Base case first. Use normal-season ADR and occupancy from real comparable listings.
  2. Add the event as a bonus line. A few weeks of elevated rates — then sensitivity-test removing it entirely.
  3. Check the safety numbers. Cash-on-cash above ~8%, DSCR above 1.25, and an IRR that still makes sense without the tournament weeks.
  4. Mind regulation. Several host cities restrict STRs; confirm you can legally operate before you buy.
If the deal only works because of the World Cup, you're not investing — you're speculating on a calendar.
