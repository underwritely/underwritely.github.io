Airbnb Investment Spreadsheet: What to Track (and What Most Miss)

Updated June 2026 · 7 min read

Search "Airbnb investment spreadsheet" and you'll find hundreds of templates that all do the same thing: multiply a nightly rate by 365 and call it analysis. The spreadsheet that actually protects your money answers a harder question — should I buy this property at this price? Here is everything that sheet needs to track, block by block, including the lines most templates quietly skip.

Block 1 — Purchase inputs (the money going in)

Start with every dollar it takes to get the keys and go live. Not just the purchase price:

  • Purchase price and your planned down payment.
  • Closing costs — typically 2–5% of the price.
  • Furnishing & setup — an STR isn't rentable empty. Furniture, linens, kitchenware, smart locks and photos routinely add five figures.
  • Immediate repairs before the first guest.

The total is your real cash invested — the denominator for every return metric later. Understate it and every number downstream flatters the deal.

Block 2 — Revenue drivers (tracked honestly)

STR revenue is two numbers multiplied: ADR (average daily rate) and occupancy. Your spreadsheet should hold both as separate inputs you can flex — never a single "annual revenue" guess.

ADR × 365 × occupancy = annual revenue. The honesty lives in the occupancy cell.

Use comparable listings for ADR, and underwrite a normal year-round occupancy (often 55–70% in a decent market) — not the best month of the best comp. If your market swings by season, add a low/high season split so a January doesn't surprise you.

Block 3 — The cost stack (where most spreadsheets fail)

This is the block that separates a toy template from an underwriting tool. Track every one of these:

Cost lineWhy it matters
Cleaning & turnoversScales with bookings; partially offset by cleaning fees, never fully.
Platform feesAirbnb/Vrbo take their cut of every booking.
Management10–25% of revenue if you don't self-manage. Model it even if you plan to self-manage — your time isn't free.
STR insuranceCosts meaningfully more than a landlord policy.
Utilities + internetYou pay them, not the guest — every month, booked or not.
Software & dynamic pricingPMS and pricing tools are small but recurring.
Supplies & restockingCoffee, soap, lightbulbs — death by a thousand line items.
Property tax + HOAFixed, and HOAs can also restrict STRs entirely — check first.
CapEx / maintenance reserveThe most-missed line. Water heaters and HVAC die. Reserve a % of revenue every month.

Revenue minus all of the above (before the loan) is your net operating income (NOI) — the number lenders and serious buyers actually look at.

Block 4 — Financing and the 6 verdict metrics

Add the loan: amount, rate, term, and the resulting annual debt service. Now your spreadsheet can compute the six numbers that decide the deal — the same six we walk through in the underwriting guide:

  • Cash flow — NOI minus debt service.
  • Cash-on-cash return — cash flow ÷ total cash invested (~8%+ is a common bar).
  • DSCR — NOI ÷ debt service; lenders like 1.25+.
  • IRR — the time-weighted return across your hold period.
  • Equity multiple — total cash back ÷ cash in over the hold.
  • Break-even occupancy — the occupancy that just covers all costs. The gap between this and your estimate is your margin of safety.

The final column: a verdict, not a vibe

A real investment spreadsheet ends in a decision rule. Set thresholds for the metrics above, stress-test the deal (occupancy −10 points, costs up, rate up), and let the sheet say it plainly: BUY, NEGOTIATE, or WALK. If the verdict flips the moment one assumption wobbles, that's not a deal — that's a hope with a mortgage attached.

Frequently asked questions

What should an Airbnb investment spreadsheet include?

Four blocks: purchase inputs, revenue drivers (ADR × occupancy), the full STR cost stack including a CapEx reserve, and financing plus the six return metrics — ending in a clear verdict.

What occupancy rate should I use?

A normal year, not a peak month — often 55–70% in a decent market. Then stress-test 10 points lower and see if the deal survives.

Is a free calculator enough?

For screening, yes. Before an offer, use a full model with financing, scenarios and sensitivity — the cost of being wrong is five figures.
